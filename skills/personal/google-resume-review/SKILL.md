# Google Resume Review

Use this workshop-informed rubric to make a technical resume specific, evidence-based, and easy to scan. It is personal guidance derived from workshop notes, not an official Google policy, recruiter scorecard, or guarantee of selection.

Read [references/workshop-rubric.md](references/workshop-rubric.md) before auditing or rewriting a resume.

## Inputs

Work with the resume the user provides. Also obtain the target role or job description and whether the application is for an internship or full-time position when those details are not already available. Do not delay the first audit if only the resume is available; inspect it, identify the highest-value gaps, and then ask.

## Workflow

1. Extract the resume's claims without changing or embellishing them.
2. Audit each rubric area as **present**, **needs stronger evidence**, or **missing**.
3. Identify information that would materially improve relevance or impact.
4. Ask focused clarification questions before rewriting claims whose ideal evidence is absent.
5. Rewrite only from verified information, then run the final quality checks.

## Evidence interview

Ask no more than five questions at a time. Prioritize questions that can materially change selection, ordering, or bullet strength. Make each question answerable; avoid broad prompts such as “tell me more.”

For a weak experience or project bullet, gather:

- the outcome or change produced;
- the metric, scale, baseline, comparison, or observable result;
- the user's specific actions and ownership;
- the technical methods, languages, or tools used;
- the users, systems, team, or operating constraints involved.

For missing resume-level information, ask for the exact field: preferred name, contact or portfolio links, degree, major or minor, attendance dates, expected graduation month and year, GPA if the user wants it shown, relevant coursework, technical skills, awards, or activities.

Continue in small batches until the important gaps are resolved or the user explicitly asks to proceed without them. Explain which section or bullet each question will improve.

Never invent a metric, technology, level of proficiency, responsibility, award, or outcome. If an exact metric is unavailable, ask about defensible proxies such as users served, records processed, latency, accuracy, revenue, cost, time saved, reliability, adoption, team size, rank, or frequency. Label an estimate only when the user confirms that it is a reasonable estimate.

## Rewriting rules

- Lead bullets with the result or accomplishment when it remains natural.
- Use the XYZ pattern when the evidence supports it: accomplishment, measurable result, and the actions that produced it.
- Prefer strong action verbs and concrete nouns; remove filler and unsupported adjectives.
- Make technical skills visible both in the skills area and in the bullets where they were applied.
- Show both classroom and personal projects when relevant. Surface awards, initiative, and a code link when available.
- Treat experience broadly. Research, teaching, tutoring, hackathons, organizations, volunteering, and technical programs can demonstrate transferable skills when formal employment is limited.
- Tailor emphasis and terminology to the target role, but use a job-description term only when the resume evidence supports it.
- Preserve the user's voice and the strongest truthful interpretation of their work.

## Recommended structure

For a student or recent graduate, default to:

1. Header
2. Education and technical skills
3. Work experience
4. Projects
5. Leadership and activities

Adapt the order when the target role or the user's strongest evidence justifies it. Aim for one page for an early-career resume when the material fits legibly; do not remove high-value evidence merely to obey a rigid page count.

## Deliverable

Before the evidence interview is complete, return:

1. the strongest existing signals;
2. the highest-priority rubric gaps;
3. the first batch of focused questions.

After clarification, return the revised resume or requested sections, plus a short list of unresolved factual gaps. If editing a file, preserve its format where practical and verify the final rendered document. Check consistency, spelling, action-oriented language, working links, legibility, and PDF readiness.
