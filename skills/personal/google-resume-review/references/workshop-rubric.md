# Workshop-derived resume rubric

This reference paraphrases Meet Bhatt's notes and photographs from a Google Careers technical-resume workshop. The source slides were marked proprietary/confidential, so the images and a verbatim slide transcription are intentionally not distributed. This summary is personal guidance, not official Google hiring policy.

## Resume-wide priorities

- Make enthusiasm for technology and the candidate's strongest technical abilities visible.
- Tailor the story to the target role instead of presenting every experience with equal weight.
- Use consistent formatting, careful proofreading, action-oriented language, and a final PDF.
- One page is preferred for many student and early-career resumes when the content remains readable.
- Treat contact information, education, technical skills, and leadership or activities as core information.
- Include awards, honors, qualifications, and projects when they strengthen the application.

## Header

- Put the candidate's name at the top; include a preferred name in parentheses when useful.
- Place relevant contact details and professional links immediately below the name.
- Consider email, phone, LinkedIn, GitHub, portfolio, or personal website according to relevance.
- Verify that every visible link works and represents the candidate professionally.

## Education and technical skills

- For a student resume, place education first.
- List post-secondary institutions and dates attended.
- State the degree plus intended major and minor where applicable.
- Include the expected graduation date as month and year.
- Include cumulative or major GPA when it helps the application and the user wants it shown.
- Add relevant coursework selectively.
- Organize technical skills into useful categories such as programming languages, tools, and concepts.
- State proficiency only when it is accurate and defensible.

## Experience and projects

- Experience includes internships, employment, research, coursework, projects, and activities.
- Write bullets around impact: what changed, why it mattered, and how the candidate caused it.
- A useful pattern is: “Accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z.”
- Quantify achievements whenever reliable numbers or defensible proxies exist.
- Surface innovation: what the candidate built, improved, automated, analyzed, or enabled.
- Include both strong course projects and personal projects.
- Mention relevant awards or recognition attached to a project.
- Name the languages and technologies actually used.
- Include a GitHub or project link when it helps reviewers inspect the work.

## When formal work experience is limited

Look for transferable evidence in:

- student organizations or professional society chapters;
- research, teaching assistantships, and tutoring;
- programming competitions and hackathons;
- classroom and personal projects;
- technical development programs;
- publications, papers, patents, or conference presentations;
- volunteering with relevant ownership, impact, or skills.

## Leadership and activities

Use this section to show what excites the candidate and how they contribute beyond required coursework. Prioritize activities that reveal initiative, sustained involvement, leadership, technical community participation, teaching, research, or service. Describe impact rather than listing memberships alone.

## Final audit

Confirm that the resume:

- clearly states degree and expected graduation month/year;
- shows the most relevant technical skills in context;
- contains impact-focused, evidence-supported bullets;
- connects experiences to the target role;
- represents broad experience rather than equating experience only with paid employment;
- is consistent, proofread, readable, and ready to export as PDF.
